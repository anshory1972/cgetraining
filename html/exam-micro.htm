A firm has CES technology with σ=0.5. If the factor-price ratio w/r rises by 2 percent, the factor intensity K/L will:

Not answered

Since KL=(δKδLwr)σ a 1 % rise in the price ratio raises intensity by σ %, so 2×0.5=1 %. “Factor intensity and factor price ratio” slide.
4

Under Leontief technology the cost-minimizing input demand is Xi=AiQ. Why does it not respond to input prices?

Not answered

With right-angle isoquants the optimum sits at the kink whatever the isocost slope. Constant returns explain proportionality to Q, not price independence. “Leontief production function is input-price independent” slide.
5

Cost minimization with CES technology gives the demand for input i. Which formula is correct?

Not answered

Demand rises proportionally with output and falls in the input’s price relative to unit cost c — not relative to another input’s price. The last option is the Cobb-Douglas consumer demand. Handout #1, eq. (9); “Demand for factors and unit cost” slide.

Which of the following is NOT a property of the Cobb-Douglas demand system Xi=αiYPi?

Not answered

Cross-price elasticities are exactly zero: demand for good i depends only on income and its own price. That restrictiveness is what motivates Stone-Geary. “Cobb-Douglas Demand System” slide.

In the Stone-Geary utility function U=∏i(Xi−γi)βi, the parameter γi is:

Not answered

γi is subsistence consumption and Xi−γi is supernumerary consumption; βi, which sums to one, is the marginal budget share. “Stone-Geary utility function” slide; Handout #2.
14

Not answered

ηi=0.1/0.25=0.4<1: a necessity, whose budget share falls as income rises — Engel behaviour that Cobb-Douglas cannot produce. “Some features of the LES demand function” slide.
